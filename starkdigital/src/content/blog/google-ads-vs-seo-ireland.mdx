## The question I get asked constantly

"Should I do Google Ads or SEO?"

The honest answer is that it depends on your situation, and on one number most people have not worked out yet. Here is the framework, with real figures from an account I run rather than the ranges everyone quotes.

## Google Ads: what it's good at

**Speed.** Google Ads drives traffic from day one. You can be visible for your most important keywords within 48 hours of launching a campaign.

**Precision.** You can target specific searches, specific locations, specific times of day. If someone types "conveyancing solicitor Dublin 4" at 11am on a Tuesday, you can be there, and only there.

**Measurability.** Every click, every phone call, every form submission, trackable. You know exactly what each enquiry cost.

## SEO: what it's good at

**Compounding returns.** SEO builds over time. A well-ranked page can drive traffic for years without ongoing spend.

**Trust signals.** Many people click organic results over ads, particularly for professional services where trust is critical.

**Lower long-term cost.** Once you're ranking, traffic is essentially free.

What that work involves in practice, from the technical side to the map pack, is on the [SEO in Dublin](/seo-dublin) page.

## Pull, push, and why that decides most of it

The useful distinction is not between Google and everyone else. It is between pull and push.

**Search is pull.** Somebody has a problem, types it into Google, and finds you. The intent already exists and you are answering it. Both paid search and SEO sit on this side: the difference between them is whether you rent the position or earn it.

**Social is push.** You appear in a feed belonging to somebody who was not looking for you, and you hope to interest them in a need they had not consciously felt. That is a different job, and for professional services it is usually the second job rather than the first.

This is why the choice between Google Ads and SEO is a question of timing and budget, while the choice between search and social is a question of what you are trying to do at all.

## What each one actually costs

For paid search I can be specific, because one account is published in full. Over ten months in a Dublin law firm, clicks averaged €2.37 and a tracked enquiry cost €51.96 on average, with the practice areas spread from €34 to €108 per enquiry. The first month cost €107 per enquiry and the most recent one cost €22. Basis: Anthony Joyce & Co. Solicitors, 25 November 2025 to 19 September 2026, and the [full account data](/blog/real-google-ads-costs-irish-law-firm) is month by month.

That is the shape of paid search: a price per enquiry you can read off a dashboard, that falls as the account learns, and that goes to zero the day you switch it off.

SEO has no cost per click at all. It has a cost per month for the work and a wait of six to twelve months before it is fair to judge, and then it keeps producing without the meter running. The management here starts at €1,500 a month for either, which makes the comparison unusually clean: the same money, one channel producing enquiries this week and the other producing them next year, indefinitely.

## What about Facebook and Instagram?

Meta is not wrong, it is a different tool, and it earns its place in three situations.

**Remarketing.** Once somebody has been on your site, from any source, staying visible to them on Facebook and Instagram costs a fraction of search. For a service with a long decision cycle this is genuinely valuable, and it is the strongest case for Meta in professional services.

**Visual and elective work.** Cosmetic dentistry, aligners, anything with a before and after. People who had not been thinking about the treatment can be interested in it, which is exactly what push is for.

**A practice nobody has heard of yet.** If there is no demand to capture because nobody knows you exist, building recognition cheaply is a reasonable first move.

Two warnings. Cheaper clicks are not better value: a click that converts a tenth as often is more expensive per enquiry, not less. And Meta's reporting claims credit generously, including for people who saw an ad and did not click it, so the two platforms' numbers are not comparable without care.

## For most professional service businesses in Dublin

If you need enquiries in the next one to three months, **start with Google Ads**. Nothing else produces them that quickly, and the figures above are what it looks like when it works.

If you are thinking twelve to twenty-four months ahead, **run both in parallel**, with ads carrying the pipeline while the SEO lands.

If you can only afford one and you can wait, SEO is the cheaper channel in the end. If you can only afford one and you cannot wait, that decision has been made for you.

SEO rarely displaces the need for Google Ads in competitive professional services markets. The best firms do both, which is why the [SEO agency Dublin](/seo-dublin) side of what I do runs alongside [Google Ads in Dublin](/google-ads-dublin) rather than instead of it.
