## The short answer

Most articles answer this with a range. I would rather answer it with an account.

Over ten months in a Dublin law firm, clicks averaged **€2.37** and a tracked enquiry cost **€51.96**. The first month cost €107 per enquiry, the most recent one €22. Management here starts at **€1,500 a month**, flat, with no long-term contract.

Basis: Anthony Joyce & Co. Solicitors, 25 November 2025 to 19 September 2026, €30,968.93 of spend, 13,083 clicks, 596 tracked enquiries. An enquiry is a tracked call or form submission, not a signed client.

That is one account in one sector, so the rest of this page is about which parts of it transfer to yours and which do not.

## What drives the cost of Google Ads in Ireland?

There are two costs to consider:

1. **Ad spend**: the money that goes directly to Google
2. **Management fees**: what you pay an agency or specialist to run the campaigns

### Ad spend: what it actually cost, by practice area

The same account, split by campaign. This is the part people are usually surprised by.

| Practice area | Cost per click | Cost per tracked enquiry |
|---|---|---|
| Immigration | €1.91 | €34.33 |
| Personal injury | — | €71.62 |
| Medical negligence | €6.89 | €107.73 |

Same firm, same city, same ten months, and the cost of an enquiry varies by a factor of three. Medical negligence clicks cost three and a half times what immigration clicks cost, because every firm in the country is bidding on those searches and they all know what the case is worth.

The practical consequence for any business with more than one service: you do not have one advertising problem, you have one per service, and running them in a single campaign means the expensive one quietly consumes the budget of the cheap one. The [full account data](/blog/real-google-ads-costs-irish-law-firm) has the month-by-month version, including the month a scaling attempt made everything worse.

For accountants, advisors and dental practices, clicks are cheaper than legal and the pattern holds: the searches closest to a decision cost the most. I do not have published figures for those sectors that I can stand over, so I am not going to invent a range for them here. Ask and I will tell you what I see in the accounts I run.

### A realistic starting budget

There is no universal number, but there is a test. A budget is big enough when a month produces enough conversions to tell a good keyword from a bad one. Below that, the account never leaves the learning phase, every report describes noise, and the optimisation everybody is paying for cannot happen.

In the account above, that threshold was comfortably cleared at around €2,000 to €3,000 a month, which produced between 40 and 70 tracked enquiries in most months. A business with cheaper clicks and a tighter radius clears it for less. One competing on the most expensive terms in the country needs more.

## Management fees: what agencies charge in Ireland

Fees in Ireland vary more than the ad costs do, and a good deal of the spread is the difference between work being done and work being reported. Percentage-of-spend models are common and they have an obvious problem: they pay more when you spend more, whether or not spending more was the right call.

At Stark Digital, management starts at **€1,500 a month**. It is a flat fee covering everything from setup to ongoing optimisation, with no hidden costs and no long-term contract, and I take on two new clients a month so the work is done by the person you spoke to.

## What budget do you actually need?

The framework, rather than a table of promises:

- **Below the learning threshold**, you are paying for data nobody can act on. Fix that before anything else, or wait.
- **At the threshold**, expect the first month to be your worst. In the account above it was €107 per enquiry against a ten-month average of €52.
- **Above it**, volume scales roughly with spend until it does not, and finding that ceiling is an experiment worth running deliberately rather than discovering by accident.

Expect at least one scaling attempt to fail visibly. In that account, April cost more than March and produced fewer enquiries, which is what a failed experiment looks like when somebody shows you the whole picture instead of the good months.

## The real question: what's the ROI?

The right question is not "how much does it cost?". It is "what is a new client worth to my business?"

Once you know that number, most decisions answer themselves. A service where one client is worth several thousand euro over the relationship can absorb a three-figure cost per enquiry without difficulty, even when only a fraction of enquiries become clients. A service where the job is worth a couple of hundred euro usually cannot, and no amount of optimisation changes that arithmetic.

That is also the honest reason I sometimes tell people not to advertise.
