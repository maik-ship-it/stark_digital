## The situation

The firm was established in Dublin and reasonably well known, and almost none of
that translated online. Enquiries arrived through referral and repeat business,
which is a fine way to run a practice until you want to plan around it. A good
month and a quiet month looked nothing alike, and neither was anybody's doing.

There had been some advertising before. What there had not been was a way to tell
which of it worked. Phone calls were not attributed to anything, the contact form
fed an inbox nobody counted, and the website asked every visitor the same generic
question regardless of what they had searched for.

## What we did

**Started with the searches that end in a hire.** Not the whole keyword universe.
The shortlist of terms someone types in the week they have already decided to talk
to a solicitor, which for this firm meant a handful of practice areas and nothing
else.

**Built a page per practice area.** Previously every campaign pointed at the
homepage, where the visitor had to work out where to go next. Each of the three
practice areas worth paying for got its own page, opening with the thing the
person had just searched for.

**Put tracking in before spend.** Call tracking on every page, form submissions
recorded properly, both tied back to the keyword that produced them. This went in
first, because a campaign you cannot measure is a campaign you cannot defend when
somebody asks whether it is worth continuing.

**Wrote the ads against the advertising guidelines.** Legal services advertising
in Ireland has constraints, and the safe version of this is to check every
headline before launch rather than after a complaint.

**Cut the waste weekly.** Legal search terms attract people looking for legal
jobs, law courses and free advice. The negative keyword list was not a one-off
setup task, it was the recurring work.

## The results

The numbers at the top of this page are from the first 90 days after launch,
measured against the 90 days before it. The basis for each is written out on the
[case studies page](/case-studies) so you can judge whether it transfers to your
situation.

The part worth noting is where the gain came from. The ads decided who arrived,
and that mattered. But most of the improvement was on the other side of the click,
in sending somebody to a page written for the thing they searched for instead of a
homepage that asked them to go looking.

## Would this work for your firm?

Honestly, it depends on two numbers: what a case is worth to you, and how many
people in your area search for it each month. For a Dublin practice in a
competitive area, both tend to be high enough. For a niche where nobody is
searching, no amount of campaign work creates the demand.

That is a twenty-minute conversation, not a proposal.
