## The firm

Anthony Joyce & Co. is a Dublin 8 practice working across four quite different
areas: personal injury, medical negligence, immigration, and notary services. On
paper that is four audiences. In search terms it is four completely separate
problems, and treating them as one account is the usual reason legal advertising
underperforms.

I have run their Google Ads for close to a year now. What follows is how the
account was sequenced, which matters more than any single tactic in it.

## Why immigration and notary went first

The obvious instinct is to start with the highest-value work. For a lot of Irish
firms that means personal injury, and it is also the area where advertising sits
under the tightest constraints. Legal services advertising in Ireland is governed
by the Legal Services Regulation Act 2015, and personal injury is where those
rules bite hardest.

So we started at the other end: the two practice areas where somebody searching
knows exactly what they want, where the copy can say plainly what the service is,
and where a page can be built and launched without a fortnight of review. Each of
those two got its own landing page and its own campaign rather than a share of a
general one.

## The notary page is the sharper example

A notary campaign could target "notary public Dublin" and compete with everybody
for a search that means almost nothing. The audience actually worth having is
narrower than that.

Irish nurses and doctors moving to Australia have to register with AHPRA, and
AHPRA requires documents certified in a particular way. Someone in that position
is not comparison shopping. They need a specific stamp, usually on a deadline,
and there are only a handful of Irish notaries set up for it.

So the page leads with exactly that: **getting your documents certified for
Australia, with the exact stamp AHPRA requires**. Same-day appointments, phone
number at the top, the Google rating and the Law Society accreditation right
underneath. The addressable audience is small enough that most agencies would
skip it. It converts at a rate a broad notary campaign never will.

## The immigration page

Same principle, wider audience. Citizenship, work permits, IRP and Stamp
renewals, family immigration. The people searching for this are frequently out of
permission or close to it, which means they want to talk to somebody today rather
than submit a form and wait.

So the page is built around the phone number, and it says explicitly that a
qualified solicitor answers the call rather than a receptionist taking a message.
That single sentence does more work than anything else on the page.

## What this looks like in practice

**One campaign per practice area, not one account.** Separate budgets, separate
pages, separate judgement about whether each is worth continuing.

**Call tracking from the start.** Legal enquiries arrive by phone. An account
without call attribution cannot be evaluated, only guessed at.

**Negatives as weekly work.** Legal and immigration terms attract people looking
for jobs, courses, free advice and the Citizens Information website.

**Copy checked before it runs.** Not after somebody raises a question about it.

## Would this work for your firm?

It depends on whether people search for what you do, and on what a file is worth
to you. For immigration, notary and conveyancing work the answer is usually yes.
For practice areas where the rules constrain what you can say, the answer is more
complicated, and that is a conversation rather than a proposal.
